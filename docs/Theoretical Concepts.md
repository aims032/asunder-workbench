# Theoretical Concepts

Continued from the 4 (0-3) laws of thermo dynamics

Six ideas carry the story, seven more carry the arc, and the rest is texture. The standard from the original guide still holds: understand each one evocatively, well enough to say it in one sentence to a ten-year-old, not well enough to defend a thesis. Then stop reading.

**How they stack.** Relativity gives the block (nothing is ever lost) and the elsewhere (nothing can be reached). Thermodynamics gives the arrow (why it feels lost anyway). The story breaks the second law to open the door and pays for it in entropy. Everything below either holds up that frame or decorates it. Sources: *Original guide* = your first research dive and reading list; *Oct 17* = the Foundations session; *New* = added here.

## Errors to avoid

Your own guide put PBS Spacetime on the list to keep you from making physics mistakes. These are the ones this story is most likely to make.

- Never add a second cheat. In relativity, faster-than-light signaling is time travel. The one door is enough.
- Entanglement sends no information. Ever.
- Quantum branching and the block universe are different pictures of reality. Use quantum mechanics as his vocabulary, never as the Asunder's machinery.
- "Quantumly transposed" is a phrase, not a mechanism. Held, not transposed.
- Entropy isn't mess. It's a count of ways. When it matters, say "ways," not "disorder."
- Relativity has no arrow. If a line needs time to point somewhere, thermodynamics is doing the pointing.

Replaces the three-month plan in the original guide. Same shape, re-aimed.

1. **Month 1, the spine.** Rovelli, The Order of Time. Chiang, "Exhalation" and "The Merchant and the Alchemist's Gate." Job 38–41. Redraw the loaf from memory.

4. **Then the pilot.** Calibration, episode one. The desk, the signal, no We yet.
